# ADR-122: Every strategy declares how its money is split; equal is the default

- **Context:** Each of the 68 deployable strategies built its own weights in code: 35 equal, 10 fixed slots, 4 with their own sizes, 13 in one instrument, and 5 that could not be checked without filings, dividends or sector data (research 140). Nothing said which rule a strategy used, so a sizing change was invisible. The operator (2026-11-02): "use equal sizing with maximum limit as default can be according to strategy if explicitly defined else from models we develop."
- **Options:** (a) Leave sizing inside each strategy. (b) Make inverse volatility the default, as the research note first proposed. (c) Make equal the default, let a strategy keep its own weights by saying so, and offer the models by name.
- **Choice:** (c). `Strategy.sizing` is `equal` (default), `strategy`, `inverse_vol`, `hrp` or `min_variance`. One function, `backtest.strategy.decide`, applies it for Tier A, Tier B and the paper/live runner. Equal splits the strategy's gross exposure, so a slot strategy's idle cash stays idle. A model's weights are capped at `max_weight(k)` inside `size_book`, so the cap also holds in Tier A. Clenow, betting-against-beta, Faber TAA, the Nifty volatility target, sandbox code and builder strategies with volatility-scaled slots declare `strategy`.
- **Why:** On five NSE baskets from 2016 to 2026, no model beat equal rupees on Sharpe by more than noise (research 140). Equal weights that are already equal come back untouched, and the six own-weight strategies keep their weights. So no stored backtest moves: the 68 strategies were checked on five dates of real data.
- **Cost if wrong:** A strategy that should keep its own unequal weights but does not declare `strategy` is silently made equal. The test `test_every_deployable_strategy_declares_a_valid_rule` names the expected rule for every builder, and an unknown rule name raises. A strategy given a model with a `lookback` under 127 rows gets equal weights, not an error.
