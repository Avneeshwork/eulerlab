# ADR-133: The brief forecasts the next session's range, close band and odds, logs each forecast and scores it the day after

- **Context:** On 2026-11-04 the operator asked for a brief with detailed reasons instead of one-liners, a forecast of the next session from the data held, each day's forecast tested against what happened, and a model that keeps updating. The brief had history base rates (ADR-109) but no forecast, no record of being right or wrong, and showed an expired option chain's straddle range (23,179–23,484) under a close of 23,811.
- **Options:** (a) Ask the model to write a forecast. (b) A direction call from a classifier. (c) Fixed statistical models from the literature (research 147): a HAR-style range model with VIX, an empirical close band, and heavily penalised odds of a higher open and close with a second stage on FII cash and the US session; each forecast appended to a log before its session, scored after it, refitted every run, and the base rate shown whenever the model trails it.
- **Choice:** (c). `forecast/next_session.py`, settings in `config/next_session.yaml`, log in `data/derived/forecasts/next_session.jsonl` (append-only, `event_time` and `knowledge_time` on every row; only forecasts made before 09:15 IST are scored). The brief adds "What happened in the last session", "Why it matters for the next session" and "What to watch" for each signal, the forecast with its reasons, scenarios and record, and yesterday's forecast against the result. The analyst note now answers four questions in two to four sentences each. S&P 500 joins the world prices for the overnight input. The brief leaves out an option chain whose expiry settled.
- **Why:** (a) cannot be scored or trusted and would invent numbers. (b) would claim a skill the data does not have: direction is close to a coin flip (T-4042, research 147). (c) gives numbers that are checked every day, with the base rate as the bar.
- **Cost if wrong:** The forecast is read as a trading signal; it says on every line that nothing trades on it and shows its own score. The settings could be over- or under-penalised; they are fixed, not tuned (A-102). About 0.7 s more per brief.
