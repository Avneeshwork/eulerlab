# ADR-209 — The idea lab and the autopilot propose new ideas only

**Context.** Operator, 2026-11-16: the idea lab and the nightly autopilot must propose genuinely new strategies, not tuned or parameter variants of ones in the library or ledger. The trial ledger holds 1,342 trials and the paper agent refuses searches once the multiple-testing budget is spent. The autopilot dropped only exact repeats (same fingerprint), so a gap fade at 2.5% after one at 3% passed; the lab spent about a third of each day on window variants (each window halved and doubled) and reverses of what predicted the wrong way; and its 13 families' templates hold 60+ formulas but only 15 distinct ideas.
**Options.** (a) Ask the model nicely in the prompt; (b) a structural idea key checked in code, plus the prompt; (c) a returns-correlation check before screening (needs a screen, so it spends what it saves).
**Choice.** (b). `research/novelty.py` gives each rule a key that leaves out thresholds, constants, windows, lags, the hold, top N, the rebalance and an event rule's ranking, and keeps kind, shape, side (with what it trades against or with), fields, operators and comparison direction. `Seen` holds the key of every card, research 176's rules, every experiment-store screen with a spec and the lab memory. The autopilot drops a card whose key is taken (and tells the model why on the retry), its fallback writes no variant, and a night with fewer new ideas writes fewer cards. The research loop's screen tool refuses a taken idea and a second screen of one idea in a run, without spending a screen. The lab no longer plans window variants or flips, takes one formula per template, and drops model formulas and research drafts whose idea it has tested. A sign is part of an idea (reversal and momentum differ; long and short differ), except the lab's exact reverse `neg(x)` of a tested `x`, which reads the same IC upside down.
**Why.** Code, not wording, decides what spends a trial; the key needs no data, so it costs nothing before a screen.
**Cost if wrong.** Too strict: a real refinement (a different window that a paper argues for) is refused and must be written as a card by the operator. Too loose: adding one extra condition makes a new key, so a filter bolted onto a tried rule still passes; the correlation "new bet" check (A-122) remains behind it. `research/evolve.py` (manual, ADR-177) still breeds variants by design and is not covered.
