# ADR-120: The capital fit caps each weight by book size and skips names it cannot buy

- **Context:** The ₹50,000 Clenow paper book held one ₹47,589 BOSCHLTD share at 89% (HISTORY #59). `fit_to_capital` ranked by weight, and Clenow's weights are inverse volatility, so the calmest stock ranked first; it then cut every name below one it could not fit. The operator (2026-11-02): "in top 10 no stock can be more than 25%, similarly in 20 stocks no stocks more than 15% weight in 5 stocks no stocks more than 35% ... ranking and weight allocation both should be respected while taking trade".
- **Options:** (a) Leave the fit alone and tell the operator to add capital. (b) Skip an unaffordable name and take the next one, as Clenow does. (c) (b) plus the operator's caps by book size, plus ranking a strategy by its own order when its weights are sizes.
- **Choice:** (c). Caps: 1 name 100%, 5 → 35%, 10 → 25%, 20 → 15%, linear between, 3/k above 20 (12% at 25, 10% at 30). Excess over a cap is spread over the others in proportion to their weights. The largest *k* that fits is kept. `Strategy.ranked_by_order` (default off) is set on `StocksOnTheMove`.
- **Why:** Replayed on the 29 Oct targets, ₹53,300 holds 19 names with none above 8%, instead of one at 89%. Every cap is at least 1/k, so a capped book is always fully investable. The 3/k tail is ours, not the operator's: it meets the 15% point at 20 and keeps any name within three equal shares.
- **Cost if wrong:** Paper and live books now hold more, smaller positions, and skip expensive top-ranked names a small account cannot buy, so they can drift further from their backtest than before. Backtests are unchanged (`fit_capital=False`); whether they should use the caps is T-4387.
