# ADR-215 — Indian papers through OpenAlex, open HTML copies read, and a replication queue

**Context.** Issue #159 (T-4659). SSRN and NBER papers were read from abstracts only; no Indian research source was searched; no card replicates a paper's own form, so a failed adaptation cannot say whether the idea or the adaptation was wrong.
**Options.** Indian sources: (a) scrape NSE, IIMA, ISB, SEBI and RBI pages; (b) RePEc or ISB's OAI-PMH; (c) OpenAlex filtered to Indian institutions. Full text: (d) add a PDF reader; (e) read `open_access.oa_url` only when it is HTML; (f) keep abstracts. Replication: (g) write replication cards automatically; (h) a report.
**Choice.** (c), (e) and (h). `india` searches OpenAlex with `authorships.institutions.country_code:IN`. `paper_search.read` tries arXiv HTML, then the open-access HTML copy (robots.txt allowed, at most 3 MB read, at least 8,000 characters and four times the abstract), else the abstract, and the paper record keeps `version_read` and `open_access_url`. `make replication-queue` lists papers whose data is here with no `source.replicates` card.
**Why.** Research 199: no Indian site has both a readable listing and terms that allow it (NSE unreachable, RePEc not for commercial use, ISB's robots.txt disallows OAI-PMH, SEBI needs permission, RBI prohibits caching and inner links), while OpenAlex is CC0 and already used. A PDF reader is a new dependency and needs the operator's yes. Cards stay the operator's or the autopilot's to write.
**Cost if wrong.** Indian working papers not in OpenAlex are missed. Most open copies are PDFs, so most reads stay abstracts. A landing page taken for the paper (A-161) overstates what was read. The queue reads routes from free-text `markets`, so it lists some papers whose data is not here (an options-based US rule) and misses some whose market is not named.
