# RBI repo rate and the monetary policy decision

*Family: Indian economy. Id: `rbi-policy-rate`.*

**In one line:** The rate at which RBI lends to banks overnight; the main lever on borrowing costs in India.

**Where you see it:** Not on the workbench yet. Events: MPC decision days.

**How we compute it:** Set by RBI's six-member Monetary Policy Committee, usually at six meetings a year. The standing deposit facility (SDF, the floor) and marginal standing facility (MSF, the ceiling) sit either side of it, 25 basis points apart in RBI's 2022 framework.

**Data and freshness:** RBI press releases on decision day.

**Do we hold it:** No. The rate is not stored; the economic calendar (ForexFactory) may list the decision as an event.

**What is normal (our data, as of 2026-10-30):** Not computed. Quote the current rate only from RBI's latest release.

**How to read it:**

- A cut lowers borrowing costs and usually helps rate-sensitive sectors (banks, NBFCs, real estate, autos); a hike does the opposite.
- Markets move on the surprise, not the decision: a widely expected cut can do nothing.
- The stance (accommodative, neutral, withdrawal of accommodation) and the inflation forecast in the statement often matter more than the rate.

**What it does not tell you:** Liquidity in the banking system (see the liquidity page); a rate can be unchanged while liquidity tightens.

**Traps:**

- Rules and calendars change; check the official page before quoting a date or a rate (A-085).
- Sources disagreed on the latest SDF and MSF levels when this page was written; take them from RBI, not a secondary site.

## Example answers

**Q:** RBI raised the repo by 25 bp. What does it mean for Nifty?

**A:** Borrowing gets dearer, which weighs most on banks' loan growth, NBFCs, real estate and autos. Whether Nifty reacts depends on whether the hike was expected; read the statement's inflation forecast and stance for what comes next.
