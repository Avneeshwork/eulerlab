# Fiscal deficit, current account deficit and trade balance

*Family: Indian economy. Id: `fiscal-and-current-account`.*

**In one line:** How much the government borrows, and how much more India imports than it earns abroad.

**Where you see it:** Not on the workbench.

**How we compute it:** Fiscal: Controller General of Accounts, monthly; targets set in the Union Budget. Current account: RBI, quarterly. Trade balance: Ministry of Commerce, monthly.

**Data and freshness:** Government releases.

**Do we hold it:** No.

**What is normal (our data, as of 2026-10-30):** n/a

**How to read it:**

- A wider fiscal deficit means more government bonds to sell, pushing yields up.
- A wider current account deficit makes the rupee depend on foreign inflows.

**What it does not tell you:** Short-term market direction.

**Traps:**

- Rules and calendars change; check the official page before quoting a date or a rate (A-085).

## Example answers

**Q:** Trade deficit widened to a record.

**A:** More dollars leaving for imports; it pressures the rupee unless foreign inflows cover it.
