# Rupee (USD/INR) and forex reserves

*Family: Indian economy. Id: `rupee`.*

**In one line:** How many rupees one dollar buys, and RBI's stock of foreign currency to defend it.

**Where you see it:** Brief World section (USDINR).

**How we compute it:** Market rate from the TradingView feed. Reserves: RBI weekly statistical supplement, Fridays.

**Data and freshness:** Brief-time snapshot.

**Do we hold it:** Rate as a snapshot; reserves no.

**What is normal (our data, as of 2026-10-30):** n/a

**How to read it:**

- A weaker rupee helps exporters (IT, pharma) and hurts importers (oil marketers, airlines).
- Foreign investors' returns are in dollars, so a falling rupee can itself trigger FII selling.
- Falling reserves while the rupee is steady usually means RBI is selling dollars to support it.

**What it does not tell you:** Whether RBI will intervene.

**Traps:**

- USD/INR up means the rupee weakened.

## Example answers

**Q:** USD/INR hit a record high. What does it mean?

**A:** The rupee is at its weakest against the dollar. It helps IT and pharma exporters' rupee earnings, raises costs for oil importers, and makes Indian assets lose value for dollar-based investors.
