# Union Budget

*Family: Government policy and regulators. Id: `union-budget`.*

**In one line:** The government's yearly plan for spending, borrowing and taxes, usually presented on 1 February.

**Where you see it:** Events; a scheduled high-volatility day.

**How we compute it:** Presented by the Finance Minister in Parliament; markets trade on the day.

**Data and freshness:** Budget documents at indiabudget.gov.in.

**Do we hold it:** No.

**What is normal (our data, as of 2026-10-30):** n/a

**How to read it:**

- What moves markets: the fiscal deficit target and borrowing (bond yields), capital spending (infrastructure, capital goods, cement), and tax changes on investors (capital gains, STT).
- Sector allocations move specific stocks (defence, railways, rural, housing).

**What it does not tell you:** Execution: allocations are plans.

**Traps:**

- Rules change; check the official circular or notification before quoting a rate or a date (A-085).
- Volatility on Budget day is high and often reverses within days.

## Example answers

**Q:** What in the Budget matters for my books?

**A:** For trading costs and taxes: any change to STT, capital gains rates or exemptions. For the market: the borrowing number (bond yields) and capital spending (cyclical sectors).
